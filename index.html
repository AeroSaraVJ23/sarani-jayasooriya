From a childhood dream to a campus aviation community

I am a Computer Engineering undergraduate at the University of Peradeniya, Sri Lanka, with interests in artificial intelligence, intelligent networks, autonomous systems and aerospace.

Leadership, to me, is not only about managing people. It is about creating a vision others can believe in, then building the structures that make it real.

Sustainable impact needs more than technical knowledge. It means finding overlooked problems, bringing people together, working through limited resources and setbacks, and building solutions that continue after the person who started them has moved on.

  1. A dream to build aircraft

    Aerospace began for me as a childhood dream.

  2. No aerospace pathway at my faculty

    When I entered university, I found there was no formal aerospace route within my faculty. I decided not to accept that limit.

  3. Learning it myself

    I studied aerospace independently through programs from Delft University of Technology, MIT and IIT Bombay, and shared what I learned through educational content and my "The Aero Girl" initiative.

  4. Founding AeroGenZ

    That work grew into the university's first aviation-focused student organization, starting with almost no resources.

  5. Leaving something behind

    I worked with stakeholders to bring aerospace equipment to the Faculty of Engineering, so the opportunity stays for the students who follow.

  6. Solving a national problem

    I co-developed SkyT, a drone-based system that supports Sri Lanka's tea plantations.

SkyT: smarter skies, greener growth

Tea is one of Sri Lanka's important economic sectors, yet labour constraints make monitoring vast plantations by hand increasingly difficult.

I co-developed SkyT, a drone-based precision agriculture system that combines UAV imaging, IoT soil sensing and AI-powered crop analysis. It makes plantation monitoring more efficient and data-driven, and it reduces dependence on manual inspection.

How the system works

IoT soil sensors

Solar and battery powered ESP32 units in the estate measure soil nutrients (NPK), pH and humidity, and pass the data to the drone over Bluetooth.

Autonomous drone

A Raspberry Pi drone flies planned missions, captures aerial images of each estate lot and collects the sensor readings on the way.

Web dashboard

Managers see each estate and lot, sensor trends and time-stamped aerial images, and can schedule drone missions from one place.

Cloud backend

Services hosted on AWS ingest the sensor data and imagery and store it securely for analysis.

  • Raspberry Pi 4
  • PX4 and ArduPilot
  • MAVSDK
